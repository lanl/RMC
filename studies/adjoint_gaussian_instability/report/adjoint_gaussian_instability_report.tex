\section{Setup}

We consider the one-dimensional Gaussian problem
\[
\pstar = N(1,0.02),
\qquad
p_0 = N(1.05,0.02),
\qquad
\sigma(t)=1,
\qquad t\in[0,1].
\]
An \emph{outer iteration} generates a new terminal distribution using the
current drift and then updates the drift from samples of that distribution.
For the learned samplers, the drift is represented by a neural network.
The first network fit uses 5000 optimization steps; subsequent outer
iterations are warm-started and use 100 optimization steps.

The practical Adjoint sampler retains previous endpoint samples in a
\emph{replay buffer}.  The no-replay ablation uses only endpoints from the
current outer iteration.  Parameter damping replaces the fully trained
proposal parameters $\widehat{\theta}_{k+1}$ by
\[
\theta_{k+1}
=
(1-\eta)\theta_k+\eta\widehat{\theta}_{k+1}.
\]
Because $p_0$ is externally prescribed rather than generated by the initial
network, the first learned update is left undamped.

For the matched-variance Gaussian model, the exact outer mean error obeys
\[
m_{k+1}-1
=
A(m_k-1),
\qquad
A
=
1+\frac{\log(0.02)}{1-0.02}
=
-2.9918602.
\]
Thus the exact update is strongly unstable.  With scalar damping,
\[
A_{\eta}
=
1-\eta+\eta A.
\]
The value $\eta=0.5$ gives
\[
A_{0.5}=-0.9959301,
\]
which lies very close to the stability boundary.  The damping coefficient
that cancels the scalar mean multiplier is
\[
\etaopt
=
\frac{1}{1-A}
=
\frac{0.98}{-\log(0.02)}
=
0.2505098.
\]
We therefore refer to $\etaopt$ as \emph{mean-optimal damping}; it is not
chosen to optimize the variance dynamics.

\section{Exact and learned affine updates}

The affine oracle uses the exact Gaussian drift associated with the current
mean and variance.  Its mean follows the predicted unstable recurrence:
\[
0.849,\;
1.437,\;
-0.312,\;
4.818,\;
-10.415,\;
34.334,\;
-98.350,\;
291.930.
\]
The variance remains close to $0.02$ throughout.  The run is stopped at
outer iteration 8 when the absolute mean error first exceeds 100.

Replacing the exact drift by a neural network changes the outer dynamics
substantially.  After the 5000-step initial fit, the first learned rollout is
close to the oracle: its three-seed average mean is approximately $0.834$
versus the oracle value $0.849$.  Subsequent 100-step warm-started fits do
not solve each affine regression problem to completion.  The resulting
iteration remains bounded for all 100 outer steps and develops persistent
oscillatory behavior rather than the geometric growth of the exact map.

Over the final 20 iterations, the learned-affine runs have mean absolute
mean error $0.348$ and average terminal variance $0.0223$.  Thus finite
inner optimization strongly modifies the unstable exact outer map even
before the reciprocal-adjoint construction and replay are introduced.

\section{Full Adjoint Sampling}

The full Adjoint sampler replaces direct supervision by reciprocal-adjoint
matching and retains endpoints from previous outer iterations in a replay
buffer.  No explicit parameter damping is used in this case.

The method remains bounded through 100 outer iterations, but the three
runs do not converge reproducibly to the same mean.  At iteration 100 the
terminal means are approximately
\[
0.903,\qquad 1.386,\qquad 0.906.
\]
Their average is $1.065$, but this average hides substantial between-seed
variation: the standard deviation of the three final means is $0.227$.
The mean absolute error over the final 20 iterations is $0.297$.

The average terminal variance over the final 20 iterations is $0.0226$,
close to the target variance $0.02$.  Thus the undamped sampler reproduces
the target scale more accurately than its mean location.

\section{Explicit damping}

\subsection{Near-boundary damping: $\eta=0.5$}

For the idealized Gaussian mean map, $\eta=0.5$ gives
$A_{\eta}\approx-0.996$ and therefore predicts only very weak contraction.
In the learned sampler with replay, however, the three runs become much
more reproducible.  Their terminal means are
\[
1.052,\qquad 1.058,\qquad 1.084,
\]
with a final between-seed standard deviation of only $0.0136$.  The mean
absolute error over the final 20 iterations is $0.0590$.

This stabilization comes with a broader terminal distribution.  The final
average variance is $0.0367$, and the final-20 average is $0.0389$, both
well above the target value $0.02$.

\subsection{Mean-optimal damping}

Using
\[
\etaopt=0.2505098
\]
produces the strongest mean stabilization in the experiment.  The three
final means are
\[
1.0092,\qquad 0.9874,\qquad 1.0131,
\]
with average $1.0033$ and between-seed standard deviation $0.0113$.
Across the final 20 iterations and all three seeds, the mean absolute error
is only $0.0105$.

The variance again converges to a value larger than the target:
the final average is $0.0370$ and the final-20 average is $0.0380$.
Mean-optimal damping therefore removes the dominant mean instability but
does not simultaneously recover the matched target variance.

\section{Replay ablation}

Replay has a strongly damping-dependent effect.  Without explicit damping,
removing replay gives a final average mean of $1.243$ and a final-20 mean
absolute error of $0.247$.  With $\eta=0.5$, replay is decisive: the
no-replay sampler has final average mean $1.811$ and final-20 mean error
$0.597$, whereas the replay sampler has final average mean $1.065$ and
final-20 mean error $0.059$.

For mean-optimal damping, replay has little effect on the mean.  The final
averages are $1.0043$ without replay and $1.0033$ with replay, and the
final-20 mean errors are $0.0098$ and $0.0105$, respectively.  Explicit
mean-optimal damping therefore dominates the replay choice for mean
stability in this example.

\section{Conclusions}

The Gaussian example separates three effects that are partially hidden in
the full learned sampler.

First, the exact affine outer update is genuinely unstable: its mean error
alternates in sign and grows at the analytically predicted rate while its
variance remains near the target value.  Second, replacing the exact
update by a warm-started finite optimization changes the dynamical system
and prevents the corresponding learned-affine iteration from exhibiting
the same explosive growth; bounded oscillations remain.  Third, explicit
parameter damping can stabilize the mean of the full Adjoint sampler.

For this target, mean-optimal damping gives the clearest mean stabilization:
both replay and no-replay variants remain within roughly $10^{-2}$ mean
error over the final portion of training and show little seed dependence.
The remaining error is predominantly a variance error, with terminal
variance near $0.038$ rather than $0.02$.  Thus damping derived from the
scalar Gaussian mean recurrence resolves the observed mean instability but
does not by itself solve the full distribution-matching problem.
